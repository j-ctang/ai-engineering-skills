**The best-supported answer: high-volume teams reuse existing demand, encode recurring maintenance policies, and split large approved goals into many tasks. They do not require a human-written spec for every PR.** Human review moves toward intent, constraints, exceptions, and outcomes.

**VERIFIED** means directly supported by the cited primary source; it does not independently establish effectiveness. **UNVERIFIED** means the sources checked did not establish the claim.

**4) Is human spec review avoidable? Per-task review often is; product judgment remains.**

- **Approve intent and invariants:** supported by pstack’s completion contracts and OpenAI’s mechanically enforced boundaries. The agent can derive implementation details.
- **Reuse templates:** pstack’s issue and verification templates reduce repeated specification work.
- **Preapprove categories:** dependency patch updates and recurring standards-based cleanup have documented automation paths.
- **Approve one program, then fan out:** Sourcegraph explicitly uses plan approval, a canary, and staged expansion. Exact instruction: “When you trust the change, ‘run everything else’ fans out the remainder.” This amortizes approval; it does not eliminate it. [Workflow](https://sourcegraph.com/docs/agentic-batch-changes)
- **Batch-approving unrelated feature specs:** **UNVERIFIED** among these sources.
- **Human PR review:** varies. Stripe retains it; OpenAI says: “Humans may review pull requests, but aren’t required to.” Neither establishes that product intent needs no human judgment. [Stripe](https://stripe.dev/blog/minions-stripes-one-shot-end-to-end-coding-agents-part-2), [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/harness-engineering/)

For a solo developer, the supported strategy is **one approved policy or goal → many evidence-backed tasks**, with agents drafting issues and decomposing programs. Your attention goes to product decisions and exceptions. **Inference:** if the application lacks users, telemetry, accumulated maintenance, or a substantial migration, there may simply be insufficient valuable work for many agents; keeping them busy is not evidence of progress.
